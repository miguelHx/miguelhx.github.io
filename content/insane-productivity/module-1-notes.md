Title: Insane Productivity: Module 1 Notes
Date: 2026-02-02 14:00
Category: Insane Productivity
Tags: productivity

Darren Hardy walks us through his life story.

He watched a program called "lifestyles of the rich and famous" and they interviewed one of the people there, asking what’s the one thing that attributed the most to your success and he said "Time Management".
He was the editor of Success magazine for 6 years, where he also got to interview the most successful people.

3 people in particular changed the way he thought about success.  They taught him that hard work wasn’t the most important thing.
He claims that there’s a type of person that can get massive results with less effort. He says the greatest achievers of our society work a lot less than we do.  They are less stressed/overwhelmed.

DH’s goal is to change our lives.  He says to mark this day on our calendars because this is the day that changes it all.  Feb 2, 2026.
This is a serious training for serious people looking for serious results.  It’s not a motivational feel good experience.

He says do not suffer the curse of knowledge.  Thinking you know it all and doing none of it.  It’s also called the fool’s syndrome, where people know a lot of information, and so they sit there and constantly go "I already know that"

Here’s the reality:  It’s not whether you know it, it’s whether you’re doing it.  And it’s not just whether you’re doing it, it’s whether you’ve mastered it.  And it’s not just whether you’ve mastered it, it’s whether your results prove and indicate that you absolutely mastered it.  And if you haven’t, there’s still something there to iterate, to tweak, to adjust, to bring to a level of mastery to where you can see the results that you want.

He says the older I get, the wiser I become the more I realize how little I know.
"People need to be reminded more often than they need to be instructed"- Samuel Johnson

The learning loop:  Hear => See => Write => Speak.  Learn as if you’re getting ready to teach this material to someone.
Handwritten notes improve speed, retention and integration of learning far superior to notes taken on a keyboard. - University of Washington

I know, I’m taking notes on a keyboard, but I think it will still help because I can keep looking back as reminders.
Many of our processes are outdated.

Ray Kurzweil, a prominent technologist, who wrote the law of accelerating returns, says there has been a tipping point that has changed progress from linear to exponential.
DH goes on to give examples of technological advancements and claims that our brains’ software is outdated.

The success gap used to be knowledge, which is why we built all these universities all over the country.
The problem is that knowledge is no longer the gap. You already know what to do.  And if you don’t, you can google it.

Ignore = not knowing. Inept = Not doing what we know.
Ignorance is excusable but being Inept is not.

So what is the success gap?  It’s conduct (behavior).
Time management isn’t the key, it’s self management.

The great battle of your life is not man against the world; it is man against the self.
Focus on character first, technology second.

Don’t seek a better app, seek a better skill aptitude.
Learning to control your attention, prevent distractions, and keep your focus.  This is one of the most important skills of the 21st century.

As a species, we are lazy. Even the very successful are lazy.  Without ambition, structure, routines, etc. most high achievers become lazy.
You need structure to drive you and guide you and keep you on the rails, in order to achieve your highest goals.  Otherwise, you can’t do it given your normal nature.

Here’s the other thing about us:  We are emotionally immature.  Our ability of self control is weak.

We have 3 brains

1. Robot
    1. Does everything on autopilot, unconscious instinct
    2. It’s only interest is survival
2. Monkey
    1. Active most of the day
    2. Emotional side of the brain
    3. Falls in love/lust
    4. Chasing shiny objects
    5. Constant stimulation
    6. Toddler side of your nature
3. Sage
    1. Makes you distinctly human
    2. Least powerful of all the brains
    3. Ability to have complex social interactions and advanced planning
    4. Seeks more, to become better


95% of all behavior is controlled by the robot brain.  Creating a habit turns it over to the robot brain.  To get to where you want in life you have to feed the Sage brain.  But the world around you is feeding the monkey brain.

We have a few jobs to do today:

1. We want to establish winning habits to turn over to the robot brain
2. Reign in the monkey. Train the monkey, take its zeal and redirect that energy in the right direction.
3. Create an environment where the Sage brain can come out, where it has a voice, attention, and a creative space where it can flourish

The sage is your superpower.  The sage is your ability to be a super achiever.  The sage is those great gifts bestowed by your creator.
The problem is that all this time and energy is going towards soliciting the time and attention of the monkey brain.
You don’t manage time; you manage emotion.

Also, we are inconsistent.  This is one of the big failures of the human species.  We get excited and motivated about something then over time we start to drift.
DH says if there’s any attribute that he’d attribute to any unusual success that he’s had it’s this one right here:  his ability to stay consistent.

In between intentions and results shiitake happens.  What we want to do in this course is provide systems and structure, to take this gap between intention and results, and mind the gap and shorten the distance between what we intend to do and the results we want.

He cannot teach us to have self control because over time willpower will fail.  We want to create systems and structures so that discipline and self control is not necessary.  We need systems to eliminate the need for discipline.

All the tools that DH is going to share with us, all the systems we’re going to build into our lives to optimize our performance and the super powers of our sage brain, come down to protecting against the weakness and redirecting the strengths of the robot and monkey brain.

Now that we know about the 3 brains and their strengths and weaknesses, we can start to work on it.

Throughout this program, DH will teach us how to prevent our robot and monkey brains from constantly de-railing us.  And most importantly, how to use the agenda and innate power of our robot and monkey brains to actually achieve the desires of the sage brain.

Instead of fighting against these powerful aspects of our brain, we’re going to redirect the power towards the aim of the sage brain.

Here’s the basic framework so that we understand why we use certain tools and why certain systems are necessary and how game changing they can be.

First, in regards to managing the robot brain.  Always remember that it has only one motive:  Survival.  It’s good at it.  But it has two downsides when it comes to supporting the dreams and hopes of that sage brain.

1. The robot brain’s OCD focus on survival drives us to be very lazy.  Change is seen as a threat, thus the robot brain creates emotional resistance.  The robot brain evokes all its powers to shut down sage brain ideas. But don’t worry, we will be turning those powers into advantages.
2. It is on constant threat alert.  If it feels fear, danger, or any other threat, it will take over the controls from the sage’s brain pronto.  Once the robot brain is on high alert and decides it wants to take over the flight controls, the sage brain gets put aside and there is nothing the sage brain can do about it

We have to build in systems to protect the robot brain from getting freaked out and yanking the joystick out of our sage brain’s hand.  And DH will show us some ways to hack the robot brain to use the fight or flight superpowers to power the agenda of the sage brain.

Now, the monkey brain, our job is to cage it or at least tame it a bit, and use its zeal and excitement, joy, and incessant desire, to our advantage.

Productivity, progress, growth, and achievement doesn’t have to be all about hard work and grinding it out.  There are ways to gamify, so to speak, the process that leverages the best of the monkey brain’s super powers while protecting it from the tendencies that usually go along with it.

That’s what we will be doing on this journey.  Equipping ourselves with the tools, and building behavior systems that will leverage the strengths of our 3 brains while protecting ourselves from those things that sabotage our progress, cause us to drift off our plan, and lead to failure on our goals.

"You can’t change the way people behave, all you can do is give them a tool, the use of which will change their behavior" - Buckminster Fuller

That is what this program will give us:  a tool.

Darren Hardy goes on to give a testimony from someone claiming that this tool was a life-changing game-changer in their life.

This module was about setting the proper foundation, changing the lens in which you see and understand yourself, why it is that you desire growth and greatness (that’s our sage brain pushing for purpose, impact, and self-actualization), and then why when you pursue these new goals and attempt these new disciplines, why you most often fail and what stops you and why.

Now that we know its due to the strength of our robot brain, and the emotional pleasure seeking nature of the monkey brain, both of which have zero interest in you doing the strenuous, no fun, hard work to obtain the sage brain’s ambitions.
With this knowledge, we can devise a new plan, a new approach to self manage and self control.

Before concluding, Darren Hardy wants to address some things.

First off, don’t get lost in the description of the three brains. This is just a simplified framework to explain what drives your behavior. You don’t really need to understand it fully.  It’s as simple as this:  95% of your behavior is unconscious - robot. Your sage brain is what brought you here, to pursue goals and betterment.

It’s your monkey brain that we have to tame and train.  This will set your sage brain, those unique superpowers that you have, those contributions that you were meant to bring to your heroic journey, free.

No monkey brain likes structure and discipline.  But it will be systems and structures that will set you free. It’s what will liberate your creativity and power your freedom.  Freedom requires structure, rules, and regulations.

So although your monkey brain doesn’t like structure and discipline, it will be your monkey brain that will benefit the most from the foundational structures that we will build into our lives.
