Where am I at now, career-wise?

Posted on Sun 14 December 2025 in Reflections

I got asked some variation of "where in your career do you think you are at?" last week during one of my final round interviews with Chime. They were gauging my level of self-awareness. I responded that I see myself as a mid-level software engineer. Wrong answer. The truth is that I'm somewhere between junior and mid-level, perhaps in the middle. But I do believe that if I get lucky enough to land a mid-level role, I can adapt to the position and fill in any gaps on the job and meet the bar.

In this blog post, I will go over my career history for some context, then we can discuss where (I think) I'm at currently.

History

I was in a Computer Science & Engineering 4-year program at UC Merced from 2015 - 2019. There, I learned the fundamentals, got an internship at pandora, and decided to join them full time as my first job out of college.

Currently in my career, I am actively seeking a new position. Had several recruiter screening interviews, with 2 of them making it to final rounds (Chime and Cash App), and 1 of them failing the first technical interview (Discord). I have a total of 3.5 years of experience.

It seems like the position I was interviewing for was mid-level, so I just said that to align with the position. After the interview, I realized this was a mistake. Reason being, is that after the interview process, if I'm honestly assessing myself, I came off as closer to junior level (and I think they might conclude the same thing). This is because when talking about my experience, I didn't have a solid project that was cross-collaborative and complex enough to meet the bar for what I think they expect of a mid-level engineer. They asked to talk about one and after thinking about it for a few minutes I just said I had a hard time coming up with one because most of my work was on an isolated part of a bigger project, or a series of smaller projects, or bug fixes. Also, my system design interview was weak, and for the programming question interview it was a backtracking problem that I got close to finishing but didn't finish. I may have gotten some brownie points if I was brutally honest with my self assessment and just admitted that I don't think I'm quite a mid-level engineer just yet, but add that I could adapt to the role if the company decides to take a chance on me.

In my first experience at Pandora, I did mainly junior engineer type work. Bug fixes, adding UI dropdowns in a flow, calling REST APIs, handling request/response cycles, writing test cases, refactoring code to use class factory pattern, translating SOAP calls to REST, working on ad targeting rule generator, etc. and lots of learning the ropes. Even if I was involved in a complicated project, I didn't play a big role in it, just worked on small isolated components. Also, I was going through mental health struggles during my time there (depression), so I wasn't all there. I admit that I was doing the bare minimum just to survive, though I was competent enough to finish my tasks (not all on time but that's normal when starting out). I ended up leaving after 1 yr and 9 months in order to focus on my mental health. I moved back in with my parents and had money saved up, so I had that safety net. 9 months later, I ended up in a better mental state and landed a job at Lively, fully remote.

In my next experience at Lively, I worked on a few important projects, but only played a small role in them, like 10-25%. I wasn't driving the projects, just doing what was handed to me. The projects included building a CRUD sign up flow for Lively's b2b product, which I only did part of the UI design implementation in React

I regret to say that I was also doing the bare minimum for the first like 25% of my time at Lively. Why? Let's just say that I was losing focus to something outside of work which was investing (chasing short term crypto gains and sports betting, which turned out to not go so well). Then after they called me out, I realized that I was doing it all wrong and decided to give it my best going forward. By this time though, I felt like they lost trust in me and started giving me less impactful/less riskier tasks.
